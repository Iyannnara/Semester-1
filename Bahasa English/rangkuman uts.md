## Module 1: Greetings and Introduction

### 1.1 Formal vs. Informal Register
Understanding social context determines appropriate vocabulary choice (*register*).

| Social Context | Salutation | Inquiring Well-being | Closing / Parting |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Formal** (Academic/Business) | *Good morning / afternoon / evening* | *How do you do?* / *How are you faring today?* | *It was a pleasure meeting you.* / *Yours sincerely,* |
| **Semi-Formal** (Colleagues) | *Hello [First Name]* | *How are things progressing?* | *Best regards,* / *Looking forward to working with you.* |
| **Informal** (Casual/Peers) | *Hey! / What's up?* | *How’s it going?* / *What have you been up to?* | *Catch you later!* / *Take care.* |

### 1.2 Self-Introduction Formulas
To communicate effectively in academic settings or IELTS Speaking Part 1, construct introductions using extended structures rather than simple isolated sentences.

*   **Origin & Background:**  
    *   *Basic:* "I am from Surabaya."
    *   *Advanced:* "I was born and raised in Surabaya, a vibrant metropolis located in East Java, Indonesia."
*   **Occupation & Academic Pursuits:**  
    *   *Basic:* "I am a student studying Business."
    *   *Advanced:* "Currently, I am pursuing my undergraduate degree in Business Administration, specializing in international marketing strategies."
*   **Aspirations & Purpose:**  
    *   *Advanced:* "My primary objective in taking this course is to enhance my linguistic competence for future academic research."

### 1.3 Introducing Third Parties
*   *Formal:* "Allow me to introduce my colleague, Dr. Aris Setyawan, who specializes in macroeconomics."
*   *Informal:* "This is my friend Maya—we’ve been studying together since high school."

## Module 2: Describing People, Places, and Objects

### 2.1 Describing People
Describing individuals requires balancing **physical attributes** with **psychological or personality traits**.

#### A. Advanced Vocabulary
*   **Appearance:** *slender, athletic build, striking features, well-groomed, distinct posture.*
*   **Personality:** *conscientious* (teliti), *articulate* (pandai berbicara), *gregarious* (ramah/suka bergaul), *pragmatic* (praktis/realistis), *introverted/extroverted*.

#### B. Grammar: Combining Modifiers
Avoid repetitive simple sentences by using relative clauses (`who`, `whose`).

$$ \text{Subject} + \text{Verb} + \text{Relative Clause} $$

*   *Example:* "Mr. Hendra is a **conscientious lecturer who always encourages** students to think critically."

---

### 2.2 Describing Places
In descriptive writing and IELTS Speaking Part 2, spatial descriptions require prepositions of place and rich sensory adjectives.

#### A. Sensory & Spatial Vocabulary
*   **Visual:** *picturesque, sprawling, congested, pristine, historic.*
*   **Atmospheric:** *bustling, tranquil, serene, vibrant, gloomy.*
*   **Location Identifiers:** *situated on the outskirts of..., adjacent to..., in close proximity to...*

#### B. Grammar: There is / There are vs. Subject Focus
*   *Basic:* "There is a park. It is quiet."
*   *Advanced:* "Nestled in the heart of the city lies a **pristine, tranquil park** surrounded by historic architecture."

---

### 2.3 Describing Objects (Royal Order of Adjectives)
When multiple adjectives describe a noun, they must follow a precise English order:

$$\text{Determiner} \rightarrow \text{Opinion} \rightarrow \text{Size} \rightarrow \text{Age} \rightarrow \text{Shape} \rightarrow \text{Color} \rightarrow \text{Origin} \rightarrow \text{Material} \rightarrow \text{Purpose} \rightarrow \text{Noun}$$

```
+-------------+---------+-------+-----+-------+-------+--------+----------+---------+--------+
| Determiner  | Opinion | Size  | Age | Shape | Color | Origin | Material | Purpose | Noun   |
+-------------+---------+-------+-----+-------+-------+--------+----------+---------+--------+
| A           | lovely  | small | old | round | brown | Italian| leather  | handbag |        |
| Several     | useful  | large | new | -     | black | Japanese| plastic  | storage | boxes  |
+-------------+---------+-------+-----+-------+-------+--------+----------+---------+--------+
```

*   *Correct:* "A **sleek, modern, rectangular silver Swiss** watch."
*   *Incorrect:* "A silver rectangular modern sleek Swiss watch."

## Module 3: Telling Time and Daily Routines

### 3.1 Advanced Time Expressions
Beyond basic digital clock readings, academic English utilizes specific idiomatic time markers.

```
       12 (o'clock)
        |
11      |      1
  \  TO | PAST /
10 \    |    / 2
    \   |   /
9----+--+--+---- 3 (a quarter past)
    /   |   \
8  /    |    \ 4
  /     |     \
7       |      5
        6 (half past)
```

*   **Exact Markers:**
    *   *On the dot / Sharp:* "The lecture commences at 09:00 **sharp**."
    *   *Quarter past / Quarter to:* 08:15 $\rightarrow$ "A quarter **past** eight"; 08:45 $\rightarrow$ "A quarter **to** nine".
*   **Diurnal Divisions:**
    *   *Dawn / Sunrise* (Fajar)
    *   *Dusk / Sunset* (Senja)
    *   *Fortnight* (Dua minggu)

---

### 3.2 Daily Routines & Adverbs of Frequency
To describe routines, use the **Simple Present Tense** accompanied by frequency adverbs and time transitions.

#### A. Scale of Frequency Adverbs
$$\text{Always (100\%)} \rightarrow \text{Usually (80\%)} \rightarrow \text{Frequently (70\%)} \rightarrow \text{Sometimes (50\%)} \rightarrow \text{Occasionally (30\%)} \rightarrow \text{Rarely/Seldom (10\%)} \rightarrow \text{Never (0\%)}$$

#### B. Placement Rules
1.  **Before main verbs:** "She **frequently attends** evening seminars."
2.  **After auxiliary/to-be verbs:** "He **is rarely** late for lectures."

#### C. Transition Markers for Sequential Actions
To ensure text cohesion (vital for IELTS Writing/Speaking):
$$\text{First / Initially} \rightarrow \text{Subsequently / Following that} \rightarrow \text{Meanwhile} \rightarrow \text{Ultimately / Finally}$$

*   *Example:* "**Initially**, I review my reading materials. **Subsequently**, I attend the morning lecture, after which I spend the afternoon conducting lab research."

## Module 4: Expressing Likes and Dislikes

### 4.1 Graduated Expressions of Preference

```
[HIGH PREFERENCE]
  │  • I am profoundly passionate about...
  │  • I derive great satisfaction from...
  │  • I have a strong penchant for...
  │
  │  • I am quite fond of...
  │  • I find ... quite appealing.
  │
  │  • I am indifferent to... (Neutral)
  │
  │  • I am not particularly keen on...
  │  • ... does not appeal to me.
  │
  │  • I have an aversion to...
  │  • I detest / I strongly loathe...
[STRONG DISLIKE]
```

### 4.2 Grammatical Structures
Expressions of preference require specific syntactic structures:

1.  **Verb + Gerund (Verb-ing):**
    *   "I **enjoy studying** foreign languages."
    *   "She **detests commuting** during peak hours."
2.  **Would rather + Bare Infinitive + THAN + Bare Infinitive:**
    *   "I **would rather conduct** online research **than spend** hours in a crowded library."
3.  **Prefer + Noun/Gerund + TO + Noun/Gerund:**
    *   "I **prefer reading** academic articles **to watching** informal lectures."

## Module 6: Giving Opinions and Argumentation

In academic tests like IELTS, stating a simple opinion is insufficient. You must **state**, **explain**, and **exemplify**.

### 6.1 Expressing Stance (Academic Expressions)

*   **Strong Stance:**
    *   *Positive:* "I am firmly convinced that..." / "It is undeniable that..."
    *   *Negative:* "I strongly refute the notion that..."
*   **Tentative / Neutral Stance:**
    *   "From my perspective, it appears that..."
    *   "It can be argued that..."
    *   "I incline to the view that..."

### 6.3 Building an Academic Argument Structure (PEEL Method)

When writing essays or delivering extended responses:

1.  **P**oint: State your main opinion/thesis clearly.
2.  **E**xplanation: Explain *why* you hold this view (cause and effect).
3.  **E**xample: Provide real-world or theoretical evidence.
4.  **L**ink: Connect the idea back to the original question.

## Module 7: Talking About Past Experiences

### 7.1 Simple Past Tense vs. Present Perfect Tense
A major focus in mid-term evaluations and IELTS grammar scoring is knowing when to use **Simple Past** vs. **Present Perfect**.

| Feature | Simple Past Tense | Present Perfect Tense |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Focus** | Action completed at a **specific** past time. | Action completed at an **unspecified** time, or connection to the present. |
| **Formula** | $S + V_2 + O$ | $S + \text{have/has} + V_3 + O$ |
| **Time Signals**| *yesterday, in 2021, two weeks ago, last night* | *already, yet, recently, for 5 years, since morning* |
| **Example** | "I **visited** London in 2019." | "I **have visited** London several times." |

---

### 7.2 Narrative Tenses for Past Experiences
When describing a detailed story or past event, combine **Simple Past**, **Past Continuous**, and **Past Perfect**.

```
PAST PERFECT            PAST CONTINUOUS                 SIMPLE PAST
(Action completed first) (Action in progress in past)  (Interrupting event)
───────▲────────────────────────▲───────────────────────────▲─────────► TIME
       │                        │                           │
  "Had studied"          "Was preparing"               "Power went out"
```

*   **Past Continuous:** $S + \text{was/were} + V_{\text{ing}}$ (Action in progress at a specific moment in the past).
*   **Past Perfect:** $S + \text{had} + V_3$ (Action that occurred *before* another past event).

#### Comprehensive Narrative Example:
> "Last semester, while I **was studying** ($V_{\text{continuous}}$) for my mid-term examinations, an unexpected power failure **occurred** ($V_2$). Fortunately, I **had already saved** ($V_{\text{perfect}}$) all my digital research notes prior to the blackout."

### Section B: Short Essay Writing (IELTS Task 2 Style Prompt)

**Prompt:**  
*Some people believe that living in a bustling modern city provides better life opportunities, while others argue that living in a quiet rural area is healthier and more peaceful.*

*Discuss both views and give your own opinion.*

#### Model Essay Outline (For Structure Study):
1.  **Introduction:**
    *   *Paraphrase Prompt:* "Whether urban living offers superior opportunities compared to the health benefits of rural areas remains a widely debated topic."
    *   *Thesis Statement:* "While rural environments promote physical well-being, I believe urban centers provide far greater academic and professional prospects."
2.  **Body Paragraph 1 (Rural Life):**
    *   *Topic Sentence:* Advocates of rural living emphasize tranquility and low pollution.
    *   *Support/Example:* Less traffic congestion leading to lower stress levels.
3.  **Body Paragraph 2 (Urban Life - Author's Stance):**
    *   *Topic Sentence:* Cities act as hubs for career advancement and advanced education.
    *   *Support/Example:* Access to top-tier universities and multinational corporations.
4.  **Conclusion:**
    *   *Restate Stance:* Reiterate that despite rural health advantages, city living remains essential for professional development.
